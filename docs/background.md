# Background: photochroms, retouching and manipulation

Short context for the question “what changed between painting and photochrom, and why?”.

## The collection

Photochroms are colour lithographs made from black-and-white photographs. Orell Füssli & Co.,
Zurich, developed the process in 1889 and kept it secret. Its subsidiary Photochrom Co. (from 1893
Photoglob) produced views of Switzerland, Europe, the Middle East and beyond, and also
**high-quality reproductions of paintings**. The Detroit Publishing Company produced photochroms
under licence for the American market (not in the ZB collection).

The Photochrom Co. handed its new series to the Stadtbibliothek, the predecessor of the ZB. This is
why the ZB holds about 10,000 prints. The publisher's sales catalogues (1891–1914) and the
*Bulletin Photoglob* are digitised on Zurich Open Publications: https://zop.zb.uzh.ch/search?query=photochrom

## How a photochrom was made

- One black-and-white negative was copied onto several lithographic stones, one stone per colour,
  each coated with light-sensitive asphalt. Sources name **4 to 20 stones** per image.
- The stones differ by exposure time and development: light colours got longer exposure (more
  tone, less drawing), dark “strong” colours shorter exposure.
- **The stones were retouched by hand**, “mostly very extensively, with chalk, scraper etc.”
  (Fritz 1894). The colours were not colour separations but **chosen freely by the lithographer**,
  probably judged from the negative alone.
- Printing order: first light, flat tone stones, last dark drawing stones.

## Why differences are expected

- **Colour:** the colours are an interpretation, not a measurement. A painting reproduced as a
  photochrom passed through a black-and-white photograph first, so all colour was re-invented.
- **Cropping and composition:** formats were cut, elements added or removed (Wegmann 2016, p. 16).
- **Taste:** colours were beautified for the market. Early editions (Venice 1889) use fewer colours
  than later reprints (c. 1897).

So the question is not only *whether* a photochrom differs from the painting, but whether a
difference is a **technical necessity**, a **convention of the process** or a **deliberate change**.
